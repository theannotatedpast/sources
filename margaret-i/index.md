# Sources: Margaret I: How One Woman Came to Rule Denmark, Norway and Sweden

*The Annotated Past* – History, told properly – every claim sourced, every source listed.

Margaret I never formally held the title of queen of Denmark, yet from 1396 Denmark, Norway and Sweden had one and the same king, Eric of Pomerania, and according to the Norwegian biographical dictionary she kept governing for the rest of her life. This video reads the titles each kingdom gave her in 1387 and 1388, from 'authorised lady' to 'powerful lady and rightful master', and puts the two documents that survive from the Kalmar meeting of 1397 on screen: a coronation letter on parchment with 67 seals, and a union letter on paper that only 10 men sealed. It then shows where historians disagree about whether the union letter was ever a legally valid treaty.
