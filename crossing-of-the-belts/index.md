# Sources: The March Across the Ice That Cost Denmark Scania (1658)

*The Annotated Past* – History, told properly – every claim sourced, every source listed.

In the hard winter of 1657-1658 the Belts froze, and a Swedish army crossed the ice from Jutland to Funen and on to Zealand. On 26 February 1658 Denmark made peace at Roskilde and ceded Halland, Scania, Blekinge and Bornholm. We follow the chain from the ice to the treaty, and test the famous story of Erik Dahlbergh: his own later accounts make his promise that the ice would hold decisive, but the oldest of his diaries says nothing about any part he played on the Great Belt, and Pufendorf's official history named Corfitz Ulfeldt as the man who, more than anyone else, influenced the king towards the crossing.
